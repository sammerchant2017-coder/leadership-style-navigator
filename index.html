Don’t treat the inventories like labels.

03 / Cross-assessment synthesis

The interesting part is where the tools intersect.

The observations below combine the instruments. Treat them as hypotheses to test with real behavior and feedback — not as a diagnosis.

At your best

  • Moves toward ambitious outcomes with visible drive and high standards.
  • Combines facts, structure and logic with an ability to rally people around a shared purpose.
  • Can organize complex work, prioritize quickly and keep attention on results.
  • Builds coalitions rather than relying only on positional authority.

When overextended

  • Pace and urgency can get ahead of other people’s processing time.
  • Directness may land as pressure, criticism or impatience even when the intent is clarity.
  • A preference for established structure can make disruptive change feel insufficiently grounded.
  • Listening can become transactional — hearing enough to move rather than enough to understand.

Useful tension #1: fast leader, deliberate changer

The Discovery profile is fast, decisive and outcome-focused, while the Change Style result is a Moderate Conserver. A productive interpretation is: “I may move quickly once I am convinced — but I want the change itself to be well-reasoned, structured and workable.”

Useful tension #2: relational influencer, passive listener

Bridging and Uniting say relationships and common ground matter. The listening score suggests the execution of listening may not always match that intention. This creates an especially high-value growth opportunity: slow the pace enough for curiosity to become visible.
